## 1. Operating rhythms

**Two jobs** (confirmed)
- Day job: finance team at Ensemble Healthcare Resource Group (Ensemble HRG): project implementations, day-to-day work, forecasting.
- Keystone AI: business development, process mapping and building, done around the day job. It also covers learning (videos, building this OS).
- Keep the day job's systems out of this OS (corporate security and auth). A lightweight day-job task list is a possible later module.

**Day-job hours** (confirmed)
- About 9:00–5:00. Can start as early as 7:30 or as late as 10:00, around fixed meetings (some at 9:00 or 9:30).
- Work from home: Mondays and Fridays; maximum flexibility (for example, start at 6:00 and stop at 2:30).
- Office days: about a 75-minute drive home. No laptop work at home after an office day. Keystone after work happens only if I started early and left early.

**Keystone time** (confirmed)
- Main block: early morning, from wake-up (about 4:15–4:30) until the day job starts. That's 1–2 hours, even on office days. I can skip a workout to get 1–2 more hours when something is urgent.
- Mornings are when I have the most energy for Keystone work.
- During the day: phone only, for approvals, quick replies, kicking off Claude Code or Codex runs, and calls (including on the drive).
- **Rule:** put anything that needs a laptop into the morning block, and keep the rest of the day to phone-sized decisions. (synthesized)

**Exercise** (confirmed)
- Goal: a baseline of health, not growth.
- Starting target: some exercise 5 days a week, of any kind; ideally 2 of those are strength sessions.
- Options:
  - Ultimate in Wheaton: Tue and Fri 5:30–6:15, Sat 5:30–6:30. Any of them, none required.
  - F3 (northwest suburbs, 5:15)
  - 30-minute zone 2 jog
  - 20-minute kettlebell (Simple & Sinister) or barbell session
- When possible, schedule hard learning within about an hour after hard exercise, when the brain is primed for learning (BDNF; idea from Dr. Lara Boyd).

**Evenings and family** (confirmed)
- Done working by 6:00, 6:30 at the latest, then present with my wife and kids.
- Family events (for example, my son's games) come up ad hoc. Plan around them on the day by starting earlier and leaving earlier.

**Planning style** (confirmed)
- Each day is shaped by fixed meetings, deadlines and family events, then by feel.
- Not yet happening: morning planning, meditation and the evening reflection. The OS should make these easy.

**Who I deal with** (confirmed)

## 2. Recurring decisions

**Where my Keystone time goes next** (synthesized, approved)
- The question I keep facing: conversations vs. building agents vs. learning.
- **Rule, in priority order:**
  1. Live deals and anyone waiting on me.
  2. Outreach to my short list of contacts.
  3. In the gaps while I wait on replies: build GTM and bizdev workflows that produce warm conversations beyond my network, and build this OS (clarity on the next best thing, and follow-through).
- Building never replaces real deal flow.
- Why: I'm not paid yet, and deal revenue is the fastest path to income. Longer term, I want to stay integral to Keystone on the way to a significant founder's stake (20–40%).
- Learning (for example, the GTM engineering course) counts when it feeds outreach or my ability to talk solutions with clients.

**Current revenue paths** (confirmed)
- Austin Linney: a super-connector with 3–4 sizable deals in the pipeline; I get 5% of that deal flow. Waiting on the builds (next few months).
- Tommy Flynn: has a few connections; I close those deals and Tommy gets a referral fee. Starting soon.
- My short list: contacts I still need to reach out to.

**Lead scoring** (confirmed)
- Every lead gets a score of low, average or high.
- Based on the dollar value of the problems they need solved, weighted toward pain they already know about and want to pay to fix.
- Includes follow-on work: a good first project often opens up the rest of their business.
- The score evolves: initial context, then AI research, then process mapping and consultative-sales conversations.
